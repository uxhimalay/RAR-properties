# Gold contrast pass — 2026-09-23

The accent read as a pale wash wherever it sat on a light surface. Measured, written down, fixed.

## How it was measured

Static inspection is not enough here: most of this page is text over photographs or video, and
walking the DOM for a background colour returns whatever is behind the picture, not the picture.
The audit therefore takes ground truth from the painted pixels:

1. Collect every element whose computed `color` is a warm tone, with its box.
2. Skip anything whose effective opacity (its own, multiplied up the tree) is below 0.98 — during a
   takeover the pixels behind it are a blend, not a surface.
3. Inject `* { color: transparent }`, which hides every glyph and changes no layout.
4. Screenshot, hand the PNG back to the page as a data URL, draw it to a canvas and read the pixel
   at each element's centre. With the glyphs hidden, that pixel *is* the element's background.
5. Compute the WCAG ratio against 4.5 (normal text) or 3 (>= 24px).

Scripts are throwaway; the method is the part worth keeping. One caveat: an element partly outside
the viewport samples a pixel that is not its own, so anything flagged should be confirmed against a
direct reading of that element's own background before it is "fixed".

## What was wrong

| Surface | Before |
| --- | --- |
| `--color-gold` #c9a962 on white | **2.25:1** |
| on cream #efede9 / #f0ebe6 | **1.92 / 1.90:1** |
| on the footer's sand #e2dacf | **1.79:1** |
| `--color-gold-light` #e6cf8f on white | **1.53:1** |
| on black | 9.34:1 — always fine, never touched |

Separately, and worse: the hero sets its copy over a photograph with no scrim. Measured against the
seeded image, **white** body text fell to **2.38:1** and both links to about **3.2:1**. That is not
a gold problem, but the gold in it could not be fixed without fixing the rest.

## What changed

1. **Two new tokens** in `globals.css`, for text and icons on a light surface:
   `--color-gold-ink: #7a5810` (6.50:1 white, 5.56 #efede9, 5.49 #f0ebe6, 4.69 #e2dacf) and
   `--color-gold-ink-strong: #5f4409` for the step darker. Same hue as the original, 41°.
   `--color-gold` keeps every dark surface, where it was already at 9.34:1.
2. **Swapped** on the light surfaces: the category eyebrow, property-card prices and their
   hover/focus border, the listings page eyebrow, filters and "show more", the property page's
   labels and bullets, the category row's hover, the white service cards, and all 30 gold text and
   border uses in the admin, which is light throughout. Gold as a *fill* was left alone: black on
   #c9a962 is 9.34:1.
3. **A hero scrim**, `linear-gradient(to bottom, transparent 28%, rgba(0,0,0,0.45) 62%,
   rgba(0,0,0,0.62) 100%)`, on both the desktop and the phone hero. Colour alone could not fix this
   because the photograph is chosen in the admin and the next one may be brighter.
4. **The property cover gradient** went from `from-black/75 via-black/20` to
   `from-black/85 via-black/45`, which lifts its gold label from 3.69:1 to 6.3:1.
5. **The glass service card** went from `bg-black/35` to `bg-black/60`: over a bright video frame
   its gold index measured 2.97:1, now 5.2:1.
6. **The focus ring** keeps its gold but gains `box-shadow: 0 0 0 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.45)`. Nothing to
   see on the dark sections; on the light ones it is what makes the ring visible at all.

Everything gold now clears AA on every surface it is painted on.

## Left alone, on purpose

`CategoryShowcase`, `ServicesRail` and `SectionVideo` choose their `initial` prop from
`useReducedMotion()`, which is false on the server and true on a reduced-motion client, so those
visitors get a hydration mismatch warning. It predates this pass and fixing it means reworking the
card-dealing animation, so it is recorded rather than changed. `StatementSection` had the same
pattern and no longer does.
